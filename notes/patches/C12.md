## 3. Old text (verbatim)
l.312: `\subsection{The boundary layer sets a floor on escalation}`

l.347 (complete):
> A case is escalated when the prediction lies within one band-width above the 0.94 pu limit, since the band straddles the threshold. The gradient-boosted model's band is only 0.0023 pu wide, but so much of the distribution sits just above 0.94 that this narrow window still captures 30.6\% of all contingencies. As the band becomes larger, escalation moves closer to every case predicted at or above the limit, which for a perfect model is 82.64\% of test cases (or the saturation point). Linear prediction predicts below the limit more often than gradient-boosted prediction, and thus has a lower escalation ceiling of 74.89\% versus 82.79\%. The gradient-boosted model flags violations about as often as they occur, so its ceiling lands just above the saturation point. A model that does not frequently predict below the threshold has a higher ceiling and misses more violations.

l.388 (sentence): "What this study contributes here is the reasoning for the existence of such a floor, and a warning that people should expect less speed-up from a surrogate model that is calibrated to be safe on average over the N-1 distribution."

## 4. What must change

### 4a. Boundary mass (C12)
- Define it **once, at first use** (title/abstract if kept there; otherwise the first body use) as a
  measured quantity: the share of converged N-1 contingencies whose **true** (solver) minimum voltage
  falls in the fixed strip [0.94, 0.945) pu — strip width 0.005 pu, fixed, not tied to any model.
  Content must include: what is counted (true voltages, not predictions), the strip, the population
  (converged N-1 rows), and that it is a property of the dataset (network × sampler × limit), not of a
  model.
- The paper currently uses **two different windows** under near-identical names (P-017 collision):
  (i) the fixed strip [0.94, 0.945) — "boundary band" (l.81), "shaded strip" (l.323), "within 0.005 per
  unit" (l.314), and implicitly "boundary layer" (l.312); (ii) the model-dependent escalation window
  [0.94, 0.94 + q̂) on **predictions** — l.347 "within one band-width". The fix needs one name per
  window and one sentence linking them: escalation = share of predictions in window (ii); boundary mass
  measures how crowded window (i) is; ρ = boundary mass / 0.005 pu is the density, and ρ·q̂ is the
  predicted escalation. ρ must be given its units (per pu) and its formula in words.
- "Throughput" (title only) is never defined; either define it as solves avoided / speedup or drop it
  from the title (P-017).

### 4b. The floor (P-014)
- "Floor" currently means two things: the **0.94 pu voltage limit** (l.107 ×3, l.303) and a **minimum
  escalation** (l.101, l.312, l.365, l.388). One of the two must get a different word; the P-017 term
  table records the collision.
- The escalation "floor" must be defined operationally **once** (which quantity, at which operating
  point), given **a value with ±**, and distinguished explicitly from the three quantities l.347
  currently gives instead:
  | Quantity | What it is | Direction |
  |---|---|---|
  | floor (to be defined) | minimum escalation compatible with the chosen missed-rate tolerance on this dataset | lower bound on cost at a safety target |
  | escalation ceiling (per model) | escalation as q̂ → large = share of cases predicted ≥ 0.94 | upper bound on cost |
  | saturation point | the ceiling for a perfect model = share of test cases truly ≥ 0.94 | reference |
  | boundary mass | share of true voltages in [0.94, 0.945) | cause/predictor, not a cost |
- Candidate definitions (the author picks one; each has a different number and a different honesty
  profile; the choice is tied to the §5 headline operating-point decision):
  - **F-a, test-picked first crossing below 1% mean missed** — histgb @0.97, ridge @0.94. Simple, but the
    operating point was read from the test sweep (Top-5 #2(d)).
  - **F-b, held-out choice (E5)** — target chosen on the inner split only; honest, but mean missed is
    above 1% for histgb.
  - **F-c, class-conditional δ = 1% (N1, threshold form on calibration violation rows)** — gives a
    nominal bound on the headline metric, no test scanning; one table row per D15.
  Whatever is chosen, the text must say the floor holds **for a single global band at this boundary
  mass**: per-element (Mondrian) calibration reaches a similar missed rate at lower escalation (E7), and
  case30 regenerated escalates 5.84%. So it is not a "must" floor (C3) — the l.101 "must" and the
  l.388 "existence of such a floor" go.
- **Checker conflict (OS-10):** `scripts/check_paper.py` bans the literal phrase "escalation floor".
  The author must decide: (1) keep "floor" as the term, define it with a number, and avoid the banned
  two-word phrase; (2) drop "floor" everywhere for a different term; or (3) ask the owner to retire the
  checker pattern (no rationale is recorded for it — `checker-phrases.md`). Do not edit the checker as
  part of this fix.
- l.347 "just above the saturation point" fails the std rule (numbers-2b 2b-5/OS-5): ceiling and
  saturation are tied. Also beware the checker's other banned phrase "lands on the saturation".
- Naming trap in the data: the JSON key for the saturation value is
  `ceilings.perfect_model_floor_saturation` — the key itself calls it a "floor". Cite the value, not the
  key's word.
- Second saturation value exists: `data/case30_frozen.json` → `case118_comparators.saturation_point_pct`
  = 82.52 (= 100 − 17.48 on all rows), vs 82.64 on the test splits. Only one may appear, with its basis.

### 4c. Heading l.312
- The heading asserts the causal claim ("sets a floor"). After the fix it must match the supported
  claim: the boundary mass predicts escalation at a given band width on this dataset (and, if E1b
  lands, is itself set largely by the N-0 acceptance filter). Wording is the author's.
