## 4. What must change
- The paper treats 1% of violations missed as the safety bar in six places and never says where it
  comes from. N-1 planning (the cited NERC standard) is a check of every contingency; NERC TPL-001-5.1
  leaves voltage criteria to the Planning Coordinator and states no numeric tolerance for missed
  contingencies (`notes/prior-art.md` §9.10). **No verified source in `notes/prior-art.md` supports a
  1% tolerance.** Therefore the only supportable framing is: 1% is the **author's chosen parameter**
  (an operator-set tolerance), stated once, at first use, with the reason for choosing it and a pointer
  to Table 2 / Fig. 2 so a reader with a different tolerance can read off their own operating point.
  (Citing a criterion would need a new verified citation — none exists; do not invent one.)
- The same 1% is used **twice** with different roles: (i) as the hyperparameter-selection constraint on
  the inner split (l.124; `tuning_search.json` → `inner_missed_ceiling` = 0.01) and (ii) as the
  operating-point bar on the test sweep (l.231, l.349). The text must say both use the same
  author-chosen value, so a reader does not think one was derived from the other.
- What "1%" means operationally must be stated once in words (ties to C4 and P-010): it is a share of
  **violations**, not of all contingencies; with a 17.48% violation rate it corresponds to roughly 0.17%
  of all contingencies being certified-but-unsafe; and at the chosen point about one test N-1 study in
  nine (histgb @0.97) contains at least one such case (P-010). The author picks which of these the
  reader needs; D15 suggests one, in words.
- It must be measured-against-labels: every missed rate is relative to the pandapower solver labels,
  which the N2 audit shows are not all consistent (P-003). Until the author settles P-003, no sentence
  may present 1% as a physical grid-safety rate.
- If the §5 headline decision adopts the **held-out** operating point, the 1% bar is not met on average
  by histgb (1.15 ± 0.27%, 4 of 5 splits above) — the text must then say so rather than keep the "just
  under 1%" framing. If **N1** is adopted, the 1% becomes the nominal δ of the calibration (a bound on
  the headline metric), which is the cleanest justification of where 1% enters — but it is still the
  author's choice of δ.

## 6. Must not claim
- Not that 1% is an industry, NERC or utility standard.
- Not that the gate "is safe" at 1%; only that the measured share of solver-labelled violations that
  were certified is about 1% on average at that point, with the split count above 1%.
- Not a physical missed rate (P-003/N2).
