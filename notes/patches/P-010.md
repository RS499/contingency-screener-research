## 3. Old text (verbatim)
l.132 (first two sentences):
> As long as the test cases and calibration cases come from the same type of condition (a single-element outage), the true voltage remains at or above the lower bound with the desired probability. If this condition does not hold (i.e., using calibration data of a single-element outage and test data where two branches fail together), there is no guarantee for what the band truly encompasses, which raises the issue of distribution shift described in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

l.363 (last three sentences):
> This establishes the lower bound because, in split conformal prediction, I can be correct on average but not necessarily inside the band. If one demands perfect correctness with a limited amount of calibration data, then the prediction intervals become extremely wide, to the point where it becomes useless \cite{barber2021}. Every base case generates 186 related contingencies, and therefore, the coverage rates are measured averages and not an assurance for a single contingency.

## 4. What must change
Two separate statistical points are currently merged; each needs its own statement, in its own place.

**(a) Exchangeability — l.132 (Method, where the guarantee is stated).**
- The condition for the guarantee is not "the same type of condition (a single-element outage)"; it is
  that calibration and test **base cases** are exchangeable draws from the same accepted-scenario
  distribution. The splits are grouped by base case (`GroupShuffleSplit` on `scenario_id`), so the
  exchangeable unit is the base case, not the individual contingency row. Rows within one base case
  share its load and generator draw and are not independent.
- Consequence to carry (content): the textbook row-level finite-sample statement does not strictly
  apply to pooled rows; the effective sample size is of the order of the number of base cases
  (300 test bases per split), not the ~55,800 test rows; uncertainty from a single split is larger than a
  row-count binomial would suggest. D15: say this in words; any design-effect number goes in a footnote
  or table note, not the main sentence.
- The N-2 shift example in the same passage stays (it is a genuine shift), but it must follow from the
  corrected condition.
- Place the E5b "missed rate is measured, not guaranteed" sentence here as well (ledger E5b; STATS-03) —
  the two belong together; coordinate so they are one passage, not two.

**(b) Marginal vs conditional coverage — l.363 (Discussion).**
- Coverage is an average over all test cases; it is not a promise for any one contingency, any one
  outaged element, or any one base case (Barber et al. — already cited). This is the point Barber is cited
  for; keep the citation attached to this point only.
- Report the measured conditional picture (E12/E13) as evidence, one sentence + number(s) (ledger E12/E13
  row, D15):
  - per outaged element at the 0.90 target, the share of elements whose coverage falls below 0.85 is far
    above what sampling noise alone would give (numbers §5);
  - operator-level: the share of test base cases (N-1 studies) that contain at least one certified
    violation at the chosen operating point (numbers §5). This is the figure that tells an operator how
    often a whole N-1 study contains a silent miss.
- The unparseable "This establishes the lower bound because…" sentence (NS-24) is replaced by content,
  not rephrased: its referent is unclear and the point it gestures at is (b).
- Keep the Manoharan contrast sentence (population bound vs per-case margin) but it must be accurate to
  the lit note: Manoharan's guarantee is a per-window bound on the skip-set violation rate, and it does
  not require exchangeability between calibration and deployment (lit note §3) — P-028 governs how the
  author may cite this after re-reading the source.
