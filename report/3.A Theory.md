Escalation happens when the model predicts in the same interval where the band crosses the threshold. This escalation behavior from the gate can be written as an equation. Let L=0.94 be the under-voltage limit, p^ be the surrogate prediction, and q^ be the calibrated one-sided conformal band width. A contingency is escalated when the prediction is inside the interval,

								[1 {𝐿 ≤ 𝑝^ < 𝐿 + 𝑞^}],

which lines up with the definitions of the gate's certify/escalate/flag mechanism as described in Section~\ref{sec:method}. The difference between this formula and the computation was 5.55 × 10^ {-17}, further validating the accuracy of the computation rather than identifying the formula as a theoretical property.

Another relationship can be drawn from analyzing both the certification and flagging conditions from the gate's method. Certification from the gate requires p̂ - q̂ ≥ L, which can be rewritten as p̂ ≥ L + q̂. In addition, a true violation results in y < L. Altogether, these give a calculation of the model's overshoot o = p̂ - y:

								[o ≥ q̂ + (L - y)],

so the model's overshoot must be at least equal to the band width plus how far off the violation was. This inequality originates from the gate's mechanisms and is, therefore, a structural property of this gate.
