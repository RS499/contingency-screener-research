To keep the comparison fair, I only test the gate's escalating feature and not both escalating and flagging because standard baseline models do not have a flagging feature. This approach shows that a simple static severity ranking, which uses only past per-element failure rates (does not read the present operating point or see any test labels), works as well as other complex models (ridge and histogram-based gradient boosting used as plain rankers) in both $k=5$ and $k=100$. For $k=5$, the tie cannot be broken, as the static order is equal to the theoretical maximum of the oracle, thus having zero headroom. In order to have a proper comparison, the budget allocated to the gate is converted to a sample size $k \approx 91.3$ for the ridge models and $k \approx 57.0$ for the histogram models. Ultimately, a fixed severity ranking performs similarly to the top-$k$ performance of tuned surrogates regardless of small or large thresholds, but for each model type, a different $k$ is generated from the budget of the gate.
